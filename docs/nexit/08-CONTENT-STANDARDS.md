# Nexit Content Standards

Nexit content must explain, contextualize, and support decisions. It must not bury meaning beneath data or use generic travel language.

## 24. Content Presentation Rules

Use:

- clear headings
- thin dividers
- checklists
- toggles
- compact tables
- concise callouts
- lightweight cards only when content needs containment
- source disclosures
- last verified labels

Avoid:

- oversized promotional cards below the country hero
- excessive rounded containers
- multiple competing shadows
- dense analytics grids
- decorative gradients
- horizontal scrolling section tabs
- generic AI-style glassmorphism
- unexplained scores
- unverified legal conclusions

### Document rhythm

Country pages should read like organized working documents:

```text
Section title
Short plain-language introduction

Key facts or interpretation

Divider

Table, checklist, or detailed explanation

Divider

Sources and next action
```

---
### Content-disclosure decision rule

For every section, ask:

> Does the user need this information immediately to understand the page, assess reality, notice a risk, or choose a next action?

- **Yes:** keep it visible.
- **No, and the content is detailed, repeated, or secondary:** place it in an accordion, toggle, drawer, or detail panel.

Never hide these behind accordions:

- page title and summary
- persistent country hero
- Nexit Match explanation
- primary cost total or budget gap
- top recommended Pathway and its match status
- plan progress, next task, and urgent deadline
- material warning or tradeoff
- legal, tax, or medical disclaimer
- source period, last-verified date, or stale status
- primary CTA
- clear empty-state explanation

Good accordion candidates:

- detailed pathway requirements
- supporting documents
- dependent rules and work rights
- application stages and renewal
- tax and legal subtopics
- healthcare-system details
- education subsections
- housing customs and portals
- family and pet import rules
- methodology and secondary sources
- plan phases and archived activity
- document categories
- FAQs
# 27. Empty States

Never invent content.

Use:

```text
Research in progress

We are still verifying this section for Portugal.
You can review the official resources below in the meantime.
```

For personalized content:

```text
Complete your Nexit Profile to receive a personalized interpretation.
```

For no saved plan:

```text
You have not created a Nexit Plan yet.

Choose a Nextination and Pathway to begin.
```

For missing Pathway data:

```text
We do not yet have enough verified information to assess this Pathway.
```

---

## Voice

- Direct, practical, informed, confident, and human.
- Explain unfamiliar terms in plain language without flattening important nuance.
- Avoid hype, generic SaaS copy, forced friendliness, and fear-based urgency.
- Never use “you qualify,” “you are eligible,” or “guaranteed” for Pathways.
- Use Nexit's approved lexicon consistently.

## Page hierarchy

```text
Title
Short plain-language summary
Key information visible immediately
What this may mean for you
Detailed research using selective disclosure
Sources, period, and last verified
Related action
```

## Greenbook labeling

Every Greenbook item is labeled as exactly one of:

- Verified resource
- Editorial context
- Community-reported experience

Do not blend categories. Experiences vary and should include date, place, and moderation status.

## Empty states

An empty state must say:

1. what is not available;
2. why it is unavailable or what has not been completed;
3. what the user can do next;
4. whether the rest of the page remains usable.
